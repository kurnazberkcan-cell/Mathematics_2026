## Exercise 15. Combining Determinant Properties

Given

$$
\det A=-2,\qquad \det B=3
$$

We need to compute

$$
\det\left(B^{-1}A^TB\right)
$$

Using the determinant of a product,

$$
\det\left(B^{-1}A^TB\right)
=
\det(B^{-1})\det(A^T)\det(B)
$$

Using the properties

$$
\det(B^{-1})=\frac{1}{\det B}
$$

and

$$
\det(A^T)=\det A
$$

we obtain

$$
\det\left(B^{-1}A^TB\right)
=
\frac{1}{3}\cdot(-2)\cdot3
$$

The factors involving $B$ cancel because

$$
\det(B^{-1})\det(B)
=
\frac{1}{\det B}\det B=1
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\det\left(B^{-1}A^TB\right)=-2
$$
